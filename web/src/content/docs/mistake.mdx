Treating <Stat n={constants.nQuestions} /> answers from one person as <Stat n={constants.nQuestions} /> independent pieces of evidence is wrong, and obviously so. People are idiosyncratic: someone who says *yinz* is more likely to say *redd up* for reasons that have nothing to do with adding new information about where they live. Counting both at full strength overstates the case twice.

So the model discounted for it. The size of the discount was not guessed — it was measured, from the residual correlation between answers after location is accounted for, and it came out at <Stat>{constants.legacyRho}</Stat>. That measurement is not in dispute. What was wrong was applying it as if the discount scaled the whole likelihood.

<Mistake client:visible />

## The discount belongs on the evidence, not on the answer

Scaling the entire likelihood does not just weaken the update; past a point it drags the posterior back toward the prior, which is a statement about where people live rather than about how anyone talks. Watch the confidence rather than the name. The best guess survives the discount — it is the same town either way — but the belief behind it collapses to a fraction of its strength, and the strongest point on the map crosses two states to New York City. The model has not become appropriately unsure. It has been talked out of what it knew.

## Why this one is worth stating plainly

The error was not carelessness. It came from doing the extra, correct thing of measuring a real effect, and then applying the measurement in the wrong place. A model with no idiolect correction at all was better than the one that shipped, and that is the model running here now.
